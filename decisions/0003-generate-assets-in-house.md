# 0003: Generate sprites and sounds ourselves instead of downloading packs

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-08
- Source: team discussion before the first showing; RESCUE_GRID_PLAN.md section 10

## Context
The game's logic must be written by the team in Java. Art and sound may come from anywhere. The first build used plain coloured squares and circles, which looked unfinished for a presentation.

Several needs pulled in different directions:
- The team wanted real-looking visuals.
- The characters are specific to the theme: a medic, an engineer in a hard hat, a dog handler.
- The team wanted to spend as little time as possible hunting for files and checking licences.

The team asked whether the AI assistant could make the assets, or whether they had to find or make them themselves.

## Options considered
- **Download free packs (Kenney, OpenGameArt, Freesound).**
  - Professional quality and a consistent style, available immediately.
  - Human-composed music sounds better than generated chiptune.
- **The team makes assets with web tools (jsfxr for sounds, a pixel editor for sprites).**
  - Fully original, and the team can say they made them.
- **Generate them with our own Java tools.**
  - Sounds come from `tools/SoundMaker.java`, a small sfxr-style synthesizer.
  - Sprites will be written as 16 × 16 character grids, turned into PNGs by `tools/SpriteMaker.java`.
  - Original, exactly on-theme, no licences, and everything can be explained as "made with Java".
  - Any asset can be regenerated by changing a number.

## Decision
Generate assets in-house with Java tools that live in `tools/`, outside the game:
- **Sound effects and music:** made now.
- **Sprites:** planned only, made when the team gives the go-ahead.
- **Placeholder art:** drawn in code with `drawFallback`, used until then, including at the first showing.

## Consequences
- **Gained:**
  - No licence tracking.
  - Characters match the theme exactly.
  - One simple story for the teacher: "everything is Java".
  - Sounds can be tweaked in seconds.
- **What we gave up:**
  - **Polish:** professional packs look and sound better than simple generated pixel art and chiptune. The music in particular is basic.
  - **Hearing the sounds before shipping:** nobody listened to the generated sounds before they were committed. The assistant can't hear them, so the team must listen and tune them.
  - **Extra code:** the tools are about 300 lines that aren't part of the graded game. They have to be explained if asked, or at least not confused with game code.
- Downloaded assets are still possible later. Dropping in a correctly named file replaces any generated one.

## Generalizes to
**Asset sourcing for small projects: generate vs. license vs. hand-make.** Generating with code wins when:
- the style can be simple (retro, pixel, chiptune),
- the content is unusual enough that packs don't match it, and
- the people presenting must be able to explain where everything came from.

This does not hold when:
- visual or audio quality is part of what is judged,
- nobody on the team can check the results by eye and ear, or
- the time needed to write the generator is more than the time needed to find a matching CC0 pack.
